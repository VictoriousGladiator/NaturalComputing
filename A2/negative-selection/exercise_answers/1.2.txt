Initially, increasing r (the number of mismatches/switches allowed when matching length-10 substrings against the English training data) improves the AUC. 
Going from r = 1 to r = 3, the AUC rises from about 0.54 (only slightly better than random guessing, AUC = 0.5) to about 0.83, 
meaning the classifier separates English from Tagalog much better.

A reasonable explanation is that with very small r the model is too strict as many English test substrings will not match well enough,
 so English strings can be incorrectly flagged as non-English, which increases false positives and hurts discrimination. 
 Allowing a few mismatches makes the model more tolerant to natural variation in English and helps it capture broader English patterns rather than only exact substrings seen in training.

However, when r becomes too large, the AUC starts to drop again. 
With high values (e.g., r = 8–9), the matching becomes so permissive that almost any substring 
can be considered close enough to something in English.
This makes the score distributions for English and Tagalog overlap heavily, so the classifier loses separation power. 
In our results, r = 9 gives near-random performance (AUC = 0.51).

Overall, the best discrimination is achieved at a moderate r = 3 , 
where the model balances being tolerant enough to generalize within English but not so tolerant that it also accepts Tagalog as English.
